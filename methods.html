01 · Clinical framework

One assessment, distinct kinds of information.

RCCKM combines published risk equations and clinical frameworks with a prespecified interpretation layer. Each output answers a different question. The platform's own Levels and Risk Signal Score (RSS) organize findings for review; their clinical usefulness and prognostic properties require evaluation in their own right.

Meaning and authority of each output
OutputQuestion it addressesBasis
PREVENTWhat is the modeled probability of a cardiovascular event over a defined horizon?Published AHA equations, with model eligibility and input-domain checks. [1]
AHA CKM stageWhere do cardiovascular, kidney and metabolic findings sit in the CKM framework?AHA's disease and risk staging framework, implemented separately from RCCKM Level. [2]
KDIGO G/A contextWhat do kidney function and albuminuria show, and is chronic kidney disease established?KDIGO classification plus explicit chronicity and certainty handling. [3]
RCCKM LevelWhich prespecified clinical pattern best describes the reviewed findings?RCCKM's ordered rule hierarchy, summarized below. A Level does not carry an event probability.
Risk Signal ScoreWhich selected findings contribute to the visible burden summary?RCCKM's fixed ordinal points and overlap rules. The total has no validated 100-point denominator.

How source evidence becomes a platform rule

Published sources inform risk equations, disease definitions, measured findings and treatment pathways. RCCKM then specifies how to reconcile those inputs, prioritize pathways and present them together. The choice to group a pattern into a particular Level, assign an RSS weight, or combine trial effects with PREVENT is a platform design decision. A citation to a guideline or trial supports its underlying evidence; it does not establish external validation of RCCKM's synthesis.

PREVENT probabilities, CKM stages, KDIGO categories, Level and RSS remain separate in the assessment. Domain-specific recommendations use the clinical findings and applicable pathways. RSS does not independently establish a diagnosis, treatment indication or target.

Proposed presentation update: probability and clinical findings

Under review, not a released change. Application draft PR #79 leads the main Next.js assessment with PREVENT probability and category, followed by separate disease/plaque findings, prioritized risk drivers and a visible input-availability cue. The legacy Level code and framework markers remain in technical details; existing exported notes, visual reports and patient handouts retain their current codes.

The proposed display does not place plaque and driver burden on a new shared severity scale. A person with CAC 5 and a person with CAC 0, severe hypercholesterolemia, diabetes and reduced eGFR can have different disease evidence, drivers and modeled probability. Neither person's position on one numbered line establishes the other's expected event risk. The legacy rule sequence below documents implementation behavior, not a calibrated ranking of those cases.

The data cue distinguishes supplied values, results needing review and values not supplied. Measured CAC 0 remains distinct from unmeasured CAC. Completed bounded or nonnumeric UACR screening remains visible without an invented exact ratio. Availability does not establish recency, diagnostic confirmation or an indication to obtain every missing test.

02 · Level logic

How the legacy rule pathway is assigned.

The engine first checks clinical ASCVD, established heart failure and quantitative coronary calcium. It then evaluates severe lipid states, kidney findings, long-term PREVENT risk and specified combinations of actionable or early signals. The first matching base rule determines the Level. A final modifier distinguishes Level 3C from 3B.

Pattern labels summarize the rule that fired. Level 3A can arise from a single-driver rule even when a 30-year estimate is unavailable or below 10%. The rule identifier and explanation supply the specific reason. Levels are prespecified categories; their ordering and boundaries have not been externally calibrated as a prognostic scale.

Information that keeps its meaning

Unmeasured CAC remains unmeasured. In this revision, numeric CAC drives the plaque-based Level 4/5 rules; qualitative incidental coronary calcium is retained in plaque context, RSS and applicable domain actions. CAC 0 does not remove a severe-hypercholesterolemia or suspected FH pathway. An isolated kidney abnormality can influence risk interpretation while CKD chronicity remains unestablished.

The 3C modifier

After any base 3B rule, the engine checks 10-year PREVENT ASCVD ≥5%, established diabetes or unconfirmed diabetes-range A1c, and the kidney-signal helper. Any positive condition changes the displayed Level to 3C, retains the base rule identifier, and records the convergence drivers separately. RSS is not an input to this modifier.

Definitions used by the counters

  • Early glycemic risk: the canonical glycemia interpretation recognizes prediabetes or unconfirmed diagnostic-range A1c. Established diabetes is a separate state. A risk signal alone does not establish a diagnosis.
  • CKM context: BMI ≥30 kg/m², OSA or MASLD, counted together as one context flag.
  • BP signal: represented BP treatment, hypertension/elevated-BP state, or at least two usable BP pairs with systolic ≥130 or diastolic ≥80 mmHg. The Level helper's BP counter is distinct from RSS's disease-authority requirement.
  • Elevated Lp(a): ≥125 nmol/L or ≥50 mg/dL in the reported unit. No fixed conversion between units is applied.
  • Kidney signal: established CKD, current G3a–G5, current A2/A3, or supported problem-list albuminuria. The compatibility path uses eGFR <60, UACR ≥30, or documented albuminuria. Current abnormal categories can trigger a signal while chronicity remains unconfirmed.
  • Counted mild signals: early glycemic risk; TG ≥150; ApoB 80–<120, or, when ApoB is absent, atherogenic findings (valid LDL-C ≥100, non-HDL-C ≥130 or TG ≥150); CKM context; elevated Lp(a); premature family history; reproductive history; inflammatory disease; HIV; South Asian ancestry; Filipino ancestry; breast arterial calcification; hsCRP ≥2; and BORDERLINE or INTERMEDIATE PREVENT ASCVD category. The lipid fallback is one flag and can overlap with the separate TG flag.
  • Actionable drivers: albuminuria, reduced current G category, established diabetes, smoking, LDL-C 160–189.999, severe hypercholesterolemia, suspected FH, BP signal, ApoB ≥100 (one ApoB driver, with a distinct label at ≥120), elevated Lp(a), premature family history, reproductive history, early glycemic risk, TG ≥150 and CKM context.
  • Metabolic-burden counter: one flag each for early glycemic risk, established diabetes, BP signal, CKM context, TG ≥150, ApoB ≥100, non-HDL-C ≥160 and valid LDL-C ≥130. These flags can describe related biology.
  • Enhancer-burden counter: elevated Lp(a), premature family history, any reproductive flag, inflammatory disease, HIV, active cancer, cancer survivorship with represented life expectancy >2 years, South Asian ancestry, Filipino ancestry and breast arterial calcification. Each is a separate Boolean flag in this implementation.
  • Hidden-risk enhancer counter: elevated Lp(a), premature family history, inflammatory disease, South Asian ancestry, Filipino ancestry, gestational diabetes, preeclampsia, early/premature menopause (one flag) and breast arterial calcification.
  • Inflammatory signal: a generic inflammatory-disease flag, rheumatoid arthritis, SLE, psoriasis, inflammatory arthritis or IBD. Reproductive flags: early/premature menopause, preeclampsia, gestational hypertension/diabetes, preterm delivery, small-for-gestational-age infant, recurrent pregnancy loss, PCOS/irregular menses and early menarche.

10-year PREVENT ASCVD categories are Low <3%, Borderline 3–<5%, Intermediate 5–<10%, and High ≥10%. In the released reference revision, the classifier retains governed unrounded precision at the 3% and 5% boundaries when matching raw model provenance is available; displayed probabilities remain rounded. The 10% boundary retains its existing reported-value behavior in that revision.

Separate numerical correction under review. Application draft PR #80 extends the same provenance-checked unrounded policy to 10%. Calculated 9.996% would remain Intermediate even when the reported numeric value is 10.00%; exactly 10% and clinician-provided 10% without trustworthy calculated provenance remain High. The equations and reported numeric values do not change. This correction can change downstream Level or action decisions and requires review of the exact clinical differences and release checks before deployment.

The separate presentation draft uses one-decimal probabilities and a category-preserving label such as “Just below 10%” when ordinary rounding would obscure the boundary. For example, 9.96% is already Intermediate under the released rules, but ordinary one-decimal display would show 10.0%. That display issue is distinct from the 9.996% classification correction. No additional near-threshold clinical category or ≥5% Level floor is introduced.

All thresholds above document implementation choices for review. The Level framework and its rule counters have not been fitted to an outcome dataset or independently validated as a new risk-prediction model.

03 · Risk Signal Score

A visible tally of selected findings.

RSS v2 is a prespecified ordinal burden score. Points reflect an intended hierarchy: plaque receives greater weight, major biologic states receive intermediate weight, and contextual findings receive smaller weights. Exact weights are RCCKM design choices informed by clinical evidence; they are not fitted regression coefficients, converted hazard ratios or probabilities.

The displayed total is the sum of displayed positive contributions. Findings can remain visible as context when a cap limits their points. Missing measurements contribute no points and remain unknown; a low total does not establish that unmeasured domains are normal.

How overlap is handled

  • Lipids: ApoB, or LDL-C ≥190 when ApoB is absent, is allocated first; suspected FH next; triglycerides last. These contributions share a 12-point cap. Lp(a) is a separate measured domain.
  • Plaque: a numeric CAC result supersedes qualitative incidental CAC. The two do not stack.
  • BP: disease authority or repeated credible elevations is required. Diagnosis, therapy and current measurement produce one contribution, up to 8 points. An isolated reading alone does not qualify.
  • Inflammatory disease and ancestry: multiple supported labels within each domain contribute once. HIV remains a separate domain; hsCRP has its own measured contribution.
  • Reproductive history: 2 points per supported marker, up to 4. Early and premature menopause are reconciled into one menopause item.
  • Kidney findings: eGFR and albuminuria are additive, reflecting separate G and A axes. Their contributions do not by themselves establish CKD chronicity.

Interpretation details

For BP, the implementation classifies stage 1 at systolic ≥130 or diastolic ≥80, stage 2 at ≥140 or ≥90, markedly elevated at ≥160 or ≥100, and severely elevated at ≥180 or ≥120 mmHg, with higher categories evaluated first. One severe current reading in established hypertension receives 6 points; 8 requires at least two markedly/severely elevated historical readings. Below-stage-1 or unavailable current BP with established disease receives 3. Clinical urgency is evaluated outside RSS.

A single hsCRP ≥2 mg/L without persistence or supportive chronic context receives 2 points. The 4- and 7-point bands require the corresponding persistence/context authority. Established diabetes or A1c ≥6.5% receives the 8-point glycemic contribution; an isolated diagnostic-range result remains subject to separate diagnosis-confirmation rules.

RSS's contextual ancestry domain represents supported South Asian and/or Filipino flags once. RSS does not use race or ancestry as a PREVENT coefficient. Contextual weighting, including ancestry, OSA and MASLD, requires further evaluation for calibration, fairness and incremental usefulness.

Reproductive items include supported menopause and menarche age/flags, preeclampsia, gestational hypertension/diabetes, preterm delivery, small-for-gestational-age infant, recurrent pregnancy loss and PCOS/irregular menses. A reproductive item adds 2 points only within the 4-point cap; all represented items remain available as detail.

RSS has not undergone external prognostic validation or formal recalibration. Its point values should be reviewed as a transparent visualization convention alongside the individual findings, PREVENT and established disease context.

04 · Simulator methods

Each scenario states how it was modeled.

Each plan forks the reviewed worksheet and reruns the clinical engine. The Level, RSS and domain interpretation reflect that scenario. The ASCVD probability comparison uses two explicitly distinguished methods.

Trial-derived LDL-C and systolic BP scenarios

For LDL-C lowering, the simulator uses the CTT 2010 relative-effect estimate of 0.78 per 1 mmol/L (38.67 mg/dL) reduction. For systolic BP lowering, it uses Ettehad 2016's estimate of 0.80 per 10 mmHg reduction. [4] [5] The engine scales each factor to the selected change, multiplies the factors, and applies them to baseline survival:

Implemented scenario transformation

f_lipid = 0.78 ^ ((LDL_before − LDL_plan) / 38.67)f_BP = 0.80 ^ ((SBP_before − SBP_plan) / 10)f_combined = product of applicable factorsp_plan = 1 − (1 − p_reference) ^ f_combined

Here, p_reference is a proportion between 0 and 1. With lipid/BP changes alone, it is the baseline PREVENT ASCVD estimate. In a mixed scenario, supported nontrial changes first recalculate PREVENT, then the lipid/BP factors are applied. Current code rounds each factor to three decimals before multiplication and reports risk percentages to two decimal places. Those decimal places describe numerical output precision; they do not quantify the uncertainty of the projection. Changes smaller than 0.5 mg/dL LDL-C or 0.5 mmHg systolic BP do not add a trial-effect factor.

The presentation draft reduces visible probabilities to one decimal while preserving underlying numeric results and audit provenance. It also removes improvement/worsening comparisons based on Level ordering. No uncertainty interval is displayed without an established method for deriving one.

The synthesis adds modeling assumptions.CTT studied major vascular events, including revascularization; Ettehad studied major cardiovascular outcomes, including heart failure. These endpoints and populations differ from PREVENT ASCVD. RCCKM treats the published relative effects as proportional hazard factors, assumes multiplicative combined effects, and applies them to the selected PREVENT horizon. This combined projection has not been directly validated in a clinical outcomes study.

Recalculated risk-factor scenarios

Supported smoking, HbA1c and UACR changes rerun the applicable PREVENT model. A new HbA1c or UACR value can affect probability only if that predictor belongs to the baseline model. The comparison preserves the baseline model and diabetes status; a change that would alter either is withheld with an explanation. These comparisons describe a modeled risk profile and do not represent the cardiovascular or kidney benefit of a particular drug.

Prespecified treatment assumptions

Starting a moderate-intensity statin assumes LDL-C and non-HDL-C decrease by 35%, and ApoB by 30%, with HDL-C held constant for the non-HDL calculation. Intensification starts from the current on-treatment lipid value. For a moderate-intensity regimen, the LDL/non-HDL multiplier is 0.50/0.65, approximately 0.769: the assumed remaining fractions of an untreated value on high- and moderate-intensity therapy. A current LDL-C of 100 mg/dL therefore becomes approximately 77 mg/dL, an additional reduction of about 23%. ApoB is multiplied by 0.80. For a represented low-intensity regimen, the current LDL/non-HDL value is multiplied by 0.50/0.80 (0.625), and current ApoB by 0.70. Lipid values are rounded as implemented. These are scenario defaults; observed response requires follow-up.

If LDL-C is absent, the selected total-cholesterol change, with baseline HDL-C held constant, can serve as a labeled non-HDL-C proxy for the lipid effect. ApoB affects interpretation but receives no additional trial ASCVD reduction. Treatment effects and direct predictor changes are separated to avoid counting the same lipid/BP change twice.

Horizon and scope

RCCKM reports supported 10-year PREVENT estimates at ages 30–79 and 30-year estimates at ages 30–59. Its current clinical eligibility policy withholds PREVENT for established clinical cardiovascular disease, established heart failure and CAC ≥300; clinicians still review the source guidance and exclusions beyond those represented in software. [1]

The scenario comparison models ASCVD. It does not project total CVD, heart failure, annual event timing, life expectancy, statin withdrawal or a complete benefit–harm balance. Carrying a trial-derived effect to 30 years assumes sustained benefit beyond the roughly five-year statin trial follow-up. Connecting lines between 10- and 30-year points are visual guides. Adherence, changing health, competing risks and treatment changes can alter an individual's course.

05 · Engineering

A shared engine, with clinician review in the loop.

  1. 1 · Reconcile the source

    A rule-based parser proposes structured values, dates, histories and medication states from supported chart text. Missing fields and conflicts remain visible.

  2. 2 · Confirm the worksheet

    The clinician reviews or edits the proposed inputs. Structured disease, measurement, treatment and certainty states carry their own meanings.

  3. 3 · Run the clinical engine

    The Python engine evaluates inputs, published calculations and explicit domain rules, producing structured results and traceable reasons.

  4. 4 · Review and communicate

    The interface presents the assessment, scenarios, clinician note and patient handout. The clinician reviews these outputs and makes the clinical decisions.

Deterministic core and optional AI

Confirmed inputs, the same engine/evidence version and the same evaluation context follow the same deterministic calculation and decision logic. Next.js, FastAPI and the retained Streamlit interface consume the shared engine's results. Renderers select and format structured clinical facts rather than independently recomputing them.

The optional Clinical Pearl is separately labeled AI commentary. It is generated from assessment context for clinician review and can vary. It does not supply the deterministic PREVENT calculation, Level, RSS or staging. Human approval remains part of using the note, diagnosis support and patient handout.

Input meaning and assessment lifecycle

The engine distinguishes absent results from measured zero, quantitative results from bounded/nonnumeric results, current treatment from documented non-use, and established disease from a finding that needs confirmation. Clinical context and observation dates matter. Input changes invalidate a stale assessment and dependent outputs, prompting reassessment.

Kidney interpretation retains G/A measurement categories separately from CKD certainty. Glycemia separates established diabetes from unconfirmed diagnostic-range measurements. BP interpretation separates a usable reading, disease authority and treatment context. These distinctions let an abnormal result prompt review without silently creating a confirmed diagnosis.

Versioning and interoperability

Result metadata includes application version, build identifier, rule-set version, evidence-map hash and generation timestamp. Rule traces expose triggering inputs and rule identifiers where implemented. A reproducible review must specify the software/evidence version, confirmed inputs, evaluation date and relevant configuration.

The current public workflow uses synthetic or de-identified text/manual entry and clinician-controlled output transfer. FHIR R4 normalization, SMART sandbox and CDS Hooks implementation are separate integration capabilities. Their presence does not establish a live production EHR connection or institutional authorization.

What the verification layers examine

  • Calculation checks: PREVENT numerical comparisons against an independently executed, governed reference implementation and supported model/eligibility contracts.
  • Clinical-rule and boundary checks: synthetic archetypes and threshold cases for disease, plaque, lipids, glycemia, kidney, BP, missing information and output wording.
  • Regression and invariant checks: changes to parsed inputs, structured results and copied reports against approved expectations. A changed expectation requires review; matching a baseline alone does not prove the baseline is clinically correct.
  • Browser journeys: ingestion, reconciliation, worksheet state, assessment presentation and copied output. The corpus includes 100 meaning-preserving format variants in addition to 500 primary cases.
  • AI semantic QA: a separately executed model reviews synthetic journey evidence using a specified rubric. Findings and infrastructure failures are retained. Model-based review has its own limitations and does not substitute for human clinical adjudication.

Do Levels or RSS add prognostic value?Independent outcome-linked datasets, calibration/discrimination and incremental-value analyses appropriate to any proposed predictive claim.

Independent prospective evidence that RCCKM improves clinical decisions, workflow efficiency, shared decision making or patient outcomes has not yet been established. PREVENT's published validation supports its source equations; it does not validate RCCKM's additional interpretation layers or the combined simulator projection.

Technical appendix C · Version binding and evidence governance

Development checks, merge checks and clinical-release checks have different scopes. Routine CI includes deterministic replay, baseline comparison and source-identity checks. Full browser/AI review is a separate explicitly invoked workflow. The presence of workflow files alone does not establish live branch protection or deployment enforcement; those settings are not certified by this document.

Full-run evidence is bound to an immutable candidate, corpus, numerical reference, contract, rubric, prompt, model and runner identity. Results from different candidates or interfaces must not be combined into a new PASS. Earlier adverse findings and their adjudications remain part of the development record, separate from later passing runs.

07 · Scope and limitations

Use the output within the full clinical picture.

RCCKM is intended to support clinician review and prevention discussions. Correct interpretation depends on the accuracy, completeness, dates and provenance of the inputs. A parser can miss or misread information, a clinical rule can omit a relevant circumstance, and synthetic testing cannot cover every patient or chart format.

Levels, RSS and simulator assumptions have their own evaluation needs. Some rules count correlated findings, and fixed weights cannot establish individualized treatment benefit. A Level name does not supply a diagnosis, a calibrated event probability or a complete treatment decision. Contraindications, adverse effects, tolerability, cost, treatment burden and patient preferences require clinician-patient discussion.

Public demonstration uses synthetic or properly de-identified information. Do not enter patient identifiers. Optional AI features have separate data-processing implications; consult the current Privacy Policy and Terms. Identifiable-data use and production institutional integrations require their own infrastructure, agreements and approvals.
